# Tata Motors: Segmentation, Targeting and Growth Strategy

**Tata Motors nearly tripled its passenger-vehicle market share (about 5% in FY20 to about 14% in FY23) by segmenting on safety and early EV adoption. Its biggest threat is weak after-sales service, and I propose three fixes; the two that can be sized are worth an estimated ₹70–120 Cr a year combined.**

## Question
How did Tata Motors use segmentation and targeting to turn around its passenger-vehicle business, and what should it do next to protect that growth?

## Data
Secondary research from public sources: Tata Motors sales and market-share data, EV sales reports (Autocar, DriveSpark), J.D. Power's 2023 India Customer Service Index, Economic Times and industry reports. Full list in the report's references.

## Approach
1. **Segmentation:** mapped Tata's four segmentation variables (demographic/geographic, psychographic, behavioural, "Vocal for Local") to target groups and models.
2. **Targeting:** separated differentiated targeting (a product ladder from Tiago to Safari) from concentrated targeting (EV early adopters, the Punch for young first-time buyers).
3. **Effectiveness:** measured the result through market share, EV leadership and brand perception, and identified after-sales service as the failure point.
4. **Recommendations:** proposed three growth moves, each with a revenue estimate.

## Key findings
- PV market share rose from about 5% (FY20) to about 14% (FY23), making Tata India's #3 passenger-vehicle maker, within 9,220 units of #2 Hyundai in H1 FY23.
- Tata captured over 73% of the Indian passenger EV market through first-mover targeting of urban early adopters.
- Poor after-sales service is the main threat: it undermines the "safety and trust" positioning the turnaround was built on.

## Recommendations
| # | Move | Estimated impact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Tata Trust Assurance": an optional ₹8,000/year service subscription for premium/EV owners, with technician pay tied to first-time-fix rate and NPS | ₹24 / 48 / 72 Cr a year at 10 / 20 / 30% uptake of 3,00,000 owners |
| 2 | Function-on-Demand software upgrades (e.g. performance boost, ADAS) sold over the air at ₹20,000 | about ₹30 Cr a year at 15% take-up of 1,00,000 EVs (₹3,000 per car on average); plus ₹15–20 Cr from anonymised telematics data |
| 3 | A cross-group "Tata Elite Cohort" loyalty tier linking car ownership to Taj, Air India and Croma benefits | Assumed 8% retention lift over five years |
