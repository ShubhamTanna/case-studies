# Code1o1: Building a Coding Academy from Zero

**Co-founded a youth-led coding academy in Mumbai and grew it to 14 paying clients and about ₹2.6 lakh in revenue with ₹0 spent on ads. The lesson: trust converts, reach doesn't.**

In April 2025 I co-founded Code1o1 with Aditya Vaishnav, a college friend. It's a youth-led coding academy in Mumbai teaching school students (Grade 5 to college) through 1:1 and small-group classes, online and at home.

## The problem

Aditya and I got into engineering because we loved building things: Arduino and Raspberry Pi robots, our own programs. Then college turned it into theory. School computer science in India is the same, so most kids lose interest before they ever build anything. Code1o1 teaches the way we wished we'd been taught: no theory-first lectures, and a real project from the first session.

## Results so far (April 2025 – October 2026)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Paying clients | 14 |
| Students taught | 16, from Grade 5 to a 19-year-old college student |
| Revenue | about ₹2.6 lakh |
| Average revenue per client | about ₹18,600 |
| Teaching delivered | about 400 hours |
| Revenue per teaching hour | about ₹650 |
| Courses designed | 11, plus a summer camp (5 students, ₹35,000) |
| Advertising spend | ₹0 |

**Current status:** active on weekends with existing students. We have paused new acquisition because both founders have other commitments (college, and my role at Yocket).

## How we got clients

| Channel | Effort | Paying clients |
|---|---|---|
| Cold calls to parents | about 500 calls → 20–30 interested leads | 4–5 |
| In-home consultations | 4 visits | 4 |
| Referrals / word of mouth | none (inbound) | 3 |
| School collaboration | 10–12 hours | 1 |
| Instagram | 20–25 hours of reels and posts | 1 |
| Tuition-centre partnerships | 10–15 centres approached, about 48 hours | 0 |

**What the numbers say:**
- **Trust converts, reach doesn't.** About 1% of cold calls became paying clients, but every in-home consultation did, and referrals came in with no effort. Tuition is a trust purchase: parents buy from people they've met or that friends recommend.
- **Partnerships failed for the same reason.** Tuition centres had no reason to trust two college students with a new product. Not one of the 10–15 centres we approached signed up.
- **The limit is teaching time, not demand.** With both founders teaching part-time, capacity caps growth. The next step is to train interns to deliver the courses, so the founders can focus on curriculum and sales.

## My role

- **Curriculum.** I designed every course and its teaching materials: what to teach, what to leave out, and how to teach it. For the advanced courses I built session-by-session plans and prompt libraries for AI-assisted session preparation.
- **Sales and outreach.** I built the lead list and wrote the cold-call script, split the calling with Aditya, and led most of the closing, including the in-home consultations.
- **Team.** I hired, interviewed and managed 3 interns (creatives, marketing, outreach) and came up with the marketing ideas.
- **Teaching.** I teach 1:1 alongside Aditya, about 4–5 hours a week.

Aditya built the website and oversaw the interns' creative work.

## Problems we solved

- **Getting listed on Google with no money.** Google Business Profile wanted proof of a physical office with permanent signage, and we had ₹500–1,000 between us. Printed posters and a flex banner were rejected as not permanent. We used spare plywood from a renovation, but laser-engraving shops refused to engrave it because the wood would burn. After asking around, one engraver layered plastic film over the wood so the laser could etch the logo without burning it. We mounted the sign, recorded the video, and Google approved the listing.
- **Finding the first parents.** Old schools and teachers led nowhere. Online parent communities (on Reddit and open parent groups) brought in our first 5–6 registrations, and word of mouth took over from there.
- **Perfectionism.** Our first website was a classmate's repurposed WordPress template. We learned to launch first and build the brand (colours, fonts, materials) as we went.

## What I'd do differently

- **Keep partnership conversations warm.** We dropped the tuition centres that said no. Staying in touch would have helped once we had more credibility.
- **Separate company money from personal money from day one.** We split the revenue 50/50 but never kept proper company books.
